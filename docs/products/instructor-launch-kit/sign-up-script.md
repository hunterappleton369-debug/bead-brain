# Sign-up script

What to say at school pickup, the texts that follow, the enrolment message, and short answers to the questions parents ask most. Read it out loud a few times. Then put it in your own words.

## The idea in one line

Every talk follows Hook, Story, Offer. The hook gets a parent curious. The story makes them feel their child could do it too. The offer makes saying yes easy. If a talk stalls, one of the three is missing.

## Rules for every talk

- Ask before you text. Only text a parent who gave you their number and said yes.
- If a parent says no, or says stop, stop. Smile. You will see them tomorrow.
- Never promise a grade, a score or a speed.
- Say "your child", not "the student".
- Talk about the class, not about money you make.

## 1. At pickup (about one minute)

### The hook

Most talks start one of two ways.

**If your child is with you:**

> "Want to see something cool? [CHILD], show [PARENT] the beads trick."

Let your child read a number off the abacus, or add three small numbers in their head. Say nothing. Let the other parent react.

**If a parent asks what you have been up to:**

> "I'm starting a little abacus class after school. It's for kids 7 to 11. Kids in Japan learn math this way. They picture the beads and add in their heads."

### The story

Keep it short. Pick one.

**Your own child (only if it is true):**

> "[CHILD] started on the beads in [MONTH]. Now [he / she / they] can [ONE TRUE THING]. And [he / she / they] asks for the flash game."

**The founder (always true):**

> "The man who made it got C's in math. He had ADHD. He trained 15 minutes a day on the beads. Around week three it clicked. Later he got a perfect ACT."

### The offer

> "It's 8 weeks, once a week, 45 minutes. Small group, 8 seats. You can try the first 4 weeks for [$ TRIAL BLOCK]. Want me to text you the details?"

If yes:

> "Great. What's your number?"

Text them before you leave the parking lot. A text sent that day gets read.

## 2. The texts

### Same day (right after you talk)

> Hi [PARENT], it's [YOUR NAME], [CHILD]'s [mom / dad / grown-up]. Here's the abacus class: [DAY] at [TIME], [PLACE]. 8 weeks. Want a seat for [THEIR CHILD]?

### Two days later (only if they did not reply)

> Hi [PARENT], just checking. I have [NUMBER] seats left in the abacus class. Want me to hold one for [THEIR CHILD]? No worries if not.

Send one follow-up, not two. If they do not answer that one, let it go.

### The day before class 1

> Hi [PARENT]! Abacus class starts tomorrow, [TIME] at [PLACE]. Bring the abacus and a pencil. [THEIR CHILD] will do great.

## 3. The enrolment message (after they say yes)

Send this as a text or an email. Fill in every bracket.

> Hi [PARENT],
>
> [THEIR CHILD] has a seat. Here is everything you need.
>
> **When:** [DAY] at [TIME], starting [DATE]. 8 weeks.
> **Where:** [PLACE, or the video link]
> **Price:** [THE OPTION THEY CHOSE]. Please pay by [DATE] with [PAYMENT METHOD].
>
> **Two things to do this week:**
> 1. Get the abacus. The free kit is at childphenoms.com/free-abacus. You pay the shipping. It comes with a short book for parents. Order it today so it comes in time.
> 2. Fill in the short form: [LINK TO INTAKE FORM]. It takes two minutes.
>
> **At home:** 15 minutes a day on the app, with you sitting beside. I will set up [THEIR CHILD]'s login and send it before class 1.
>
> **Missed a class?** No problem. [THEIR CHILD] can catch up at home on the same two lessons.
>
> See you on [DATE]!
> [YOUR NAME]

## 4. When a parent pushes back

Most "no" answers come from one of three beliefs. Each one has a short answer. Say it, then stop talking. Let them think.

### "School already teaches math. [Child] is fine."

> "I'm sure [he / she] is. School teaches the same lesson to every kid in the room. This is a different skill. Your child learns to see the numbers as beads and work them in their head. School does not teach that."

If they want more:

> "On the last national test, only 28% of US 8th graders scored proficient in math. I wanted something extra for mine."

### "[Child] is not a math kid." / "I'm not a math person."

> "That's what they said about the man who made this. C's in math. ADHD. Could not sit still. He trained 15 minutes a day, and it clicked around week three."

> "And you don't have to teach it. I teach it in class. At home, you sit beside for 15 minutes. The app picks the problems and checks them."

For a fidgety child:

> "Kids who can't sit still are welcome. The math is in their hands first."

### "We don't have time." / "It's too much money." / "Doesn't that take years?"

**Time:**

> "It's one class a week. At home it's 15 minutes a day. That's less than one drive to a tutor."

**Money:**

> "A tutor is about $40 to $80 an hour. This is [$ PER CLASS] a class, in a group with friends. And you can try 4 weeks first."

**Takes years:**

> "The full path takes years, like piano. But in 8 weeks your child learns to read and set the beads and add on them. They also start doing small sums in their head. You'll see it at the last class."

### "Does it really work?"

> "A 3-year study in classrooms in India tested it. Kids who did the abacus beat kids who did extra math, on adding and on place value. It's not magic. It's practice, done the right way."

### "My child already does Kumon."

> "That's fine. Lots of families do both. Kumon is mostly worksheets. This is beads and pictures in the head. Try the first 4 weeks and see if [he / she] likes it."

## 5. At the last class (the next step)

Families are in the room for the family flash round. That is the best moment to ask.

> "Thank you for coming. Next comes Stage 3. That's where they learn to carry and borrow on the beads. The next class starts [DATE]. Want me to hold [THEIR CHILD]'s seat?"

For families who want more practice at home:

> "If you want more for home, the free kit page leads to Phenom University. It has a free 14-day test drive, and it covers every child in your house."

Say it once. Do not push it.
