# Pricing sheet

How to set your price for the 8-week starter course, the options to offer, and the plain math behind them. Your rates are yours to set.

## The range

Most instructors land between **$20 and $40 a session** per child (the figure on the certification page). Where you sit in that range depends on your area, the room, the group size and how much you want the class to be a small business or a favour to friends. Some instructors teach for free. All of these are fine.

A few anchors help when a parent asks how your price compares. Kumon-style centers run about $100 to $250 a month per subject, and private math tutors charge about $40 to $80 an hour. In a 2025 EdChoice survey, parents said they would pay about $357 a month for tutoring ([EdChoice](https://www.edchoice.org/2025-a-kaleidoscope-view-of-k12-tutoring-in-america/)). A weekly class at $30 comes to about $120 to $150 a month. You do not need to say any of this out loud unless a parent asks. It is here so you can set your price with a straight face.

## The four options

Offer two or three of these, not all four. Too many choices slow a parent down.

### 1. Pay per class

The parent pays your session rate each week.

- Good for: a first class with families you do not know yet.
- Downside: kids drop off after a busy week, and the group shrinks.

### 2. The full course, paid up front

The parent pays for all eight classes before week 1. Give a reason to choose it: one class free for paying in full is simple and easy to explain.

| Session rate | 8 classes, paid weekly | Full course, one class free |
|---|---|---|
| $20 | $160 | $140 |
| $25 | $200 | $175 |
| $30 | $240 | $210 |
| $40 | $320 | $280 |

This is the option to lead with. Kids who come every week get the most practice, and paying up front gives a family a reason to come.

### 3. Sibling price

A second child from the same house pays less, because the family is already driving over and already practising at home. A common choice is 25% off the second child. Pick one rule and keep it for every family.

- Example at $30 a session: the first child is $30, the second is $22.50 a class (or $157.50 for the full course with one class free).

### 4. The 4-week trial block

Weeks 1 to 4 cover all of Stage 1 (Sprout) and end with the Sprout Boss and a written progress report. That is a natural point for a family to decide. Price the block at four times your session rate, paid up front.

- Example at $30: $120 for weeks 1 to 4.
- At week 4, offer weeks 5 to 8 at the same rate, or credit the trial toward the full-course price so the family pays the difference: at $30, that is $210 − $120 = $90 for weeks 5 to 8.
- Hold the trial seat only until week 2. After that, a new child is too far behind to join the group.

## The math for 6 kids over 8 weeks

Here is the arithmetic for one group of six children who each come to all eight classes, at three points in the range. It is gross class fees only, before any of your costs, taxes or time. It is an example of how to do the sum, not a forecast of what you will make.

| Session rate | Per child (8 classes) | Group of 6 |
|---|---|---|
| $20 | $160 | $960 |
| $30 | $240 | $1,440 |
| $40 | $320 | $1,920 |

The sum is: rate × 8 classes × 6 children. If two families take the full-course price (one class free), take off one class for each of them. If one child is a sibling at 25% off, take off a quarter of that child's total.

**Worked example at $30, with real-life changes.** Four children pay the full course at $210 each ($840). One pays weekly and comes to seven classes ($210). One is a sibling on the full course at 25% off ($157.50). Gross: $840 + $210 + $157.50 = **$1,207.50**.

## How to take payment

- Collect before the first class, not at the door. A family that has paid shows up.
- Use one method for everyone: an invoice, a payment app or a payment link. Put the method in the enrolment message (`sign-up-script.md`).
- Write down your missed-class rule before you need it. A simple one: missed classes are not refunded, and the child catches up on the same two lessons in the app at home. If you cancel a class, you refund it or run a make-up.

## What not to say

- Do not tell a parent what their child will score, or how fast they will learn. Say what the class covers and what the child will be able to try.
- Do not tell anyone how much you earn or will earn from teaching. If another parent asks about becoming an instructor, point them to the certification page and let them judge for themselves.
