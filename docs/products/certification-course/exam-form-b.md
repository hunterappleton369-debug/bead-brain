# Certified Phenom Instructor exam (retake form B)

Forty questions in three parts. Sixty minutes. Pass with 32 of 40 and no part below its floor.

## Before you start

- **When to take it.** On demand, after Lesson 20, when the drills in Lessons 9, 15 and 16 feel calm.
- **Time.** 60 minutes for all three parts. Work in any order.
- **What you may use.** Your soroban for Parts A and B. A pencil and this paper. Nothing else.
- **What you may not use.** A calculator, the trainer, the course notes, or another person. Question 24 is mental: no soroban and no paper for that one.
- **How to answer.** Part A: name the move and write its spoken line. Part B: write the answer (and, for 25 and 26, every rod move in order). Part C: circle one letter.

## How it is marked

| Part | What it tests | Questions | Floor |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Bead-move classification | 1–12 | 9 of 12 |
| B | Worked problems | 13–26 | 10 of 14 |
| C | Teaching scenarios | 27–40 | 10 of 14 |
| All | Pass mark | 40 | 32 of 40 (80%) |

One point per question. No half points. You pass when your total is 32 or more **and** every part meets its floor. If you miss, you get your score for each part and can retake whenever you are ready, on a fresh form. After a second miss, the Pass Promise starts: one-on-one coaching until you pass (see `pass-promise-ops.md`).

## Part C · Teaching scenarios (questions 27–40)

Circle the one best answer.

27. A parent at pickup asks, "Will this help my son's ADHD?" What do you say?
    - A. "Yes. Hunter had ADHD, and look what happened to him."
    - B. "Studies show it improves focus and attention."
    - C. "I can't promise anything about ADHD, and the beads aren't a treatment. What the method builds is arithmetic speed and accuracy, fifteen minutes a day."
    - D. "It works for every child, so it will work for him."

28. A Stage 2 child's rod shows 4. The next line is +1. She freezes for ten seconds. What do you do?
    - A. Ask "Free beads, or friend?" and wait.
    - B. Say "Bring the five down and take away four."
    - C. Move the beads slowly so she can watch.
    - D. Skip the problem so she doesn't get upset.

29. A child gets 7 of 10 on the speed round, fast. What do you do?
    - A. Push for 10 of 10 at the same speed tomorrow.
    - B. Say "slow is fine," time nothing for a week, and bring the clock back when accuracy is 9 of 10.
    - C. Move on to the next lesson, since 7 is close.
    - D. Add more problems to the drill so the child gets more practice.

30. A child takes the Explorer boss. Parts one and three pass. Part two (2-digit, four lines) scores 4 of 6. What next?
    - A. Restart Stage 2 from the first lesson.
    - B. Retry the boss right away while the moves are fresh.
    - C. Open Stage 3, since two of three parts passed.
    - D. Spend three days on the 2-digit small-friend lessons, then retry the boss.

31. A child clears rung 11 of the Bead Score. Her father asks, "So she's 7th kyu now?" What do you say?
    - A. "Yes, she's officially 7th kyu."
    - B. "She cleared rung 11. That's about 7th kyu on the Japanese flash ladder. We're set against those grades, but we don't award them."
    - C. "Kyu grades don't mean anything."
    - D. "She'll be 1st kyu by the end of the year."

32. A parent asks whether she could teach this too, and how much instructors make. What may you say?
    - A. "Most instructors land between $20 and $40 a session. Your rates are yours to set."
    - B. "You can easily make $2,000 a month."
    - C. "Enough to pay for your child's lessons, guaranteed."
    - D. "Most instructors replace their full-time income in a year."

33. In a flash round, a child loses track at the fourth number. What do you say?
    - A. "Try to remember the numbers I said."
    - B. "Let's do that one again, slower."
    - C. "Let it go. Next one."
    - D. "You need to concentrate harder."

34. Halfway through a mental sum, a child says, "I lost it." What is the fix?
    - A. Give the answer so the child can move on.
    - B. Tell the child to say the running total out loud.
    - C. Make the next round shorter.
    - D. Do the same sum once on the real soroban, close eyes, replay it, then the next one in the head.

35. When do you speed up the flash tempo for a child?
    - A. When the child is right nine times out of ten at the current tempo.
    - B. Every week, on a fixed schedule.
    - C. When the child asks to go faster.
    - D. When the child is right half the time.

36. A Stage 1 child takes six seconds to read 47 and her lips move as she looks at the beads. What do you do?
    - A. Read the number for her, then have her repeat it.
    - B. Use the peek: show the number for about two seconds, then cover it.
    - C. Let her count, since speed comes later.
    - D. Move her on to adding.

37. Your class has three kids at Stage 2 and three at Stage 3. How do you run it?
    - A. Teach the Stage 3 lesson to everyone, since it's harder.
    - B. Teach the Stage 2 lesson to everyone, since it's easier.
    - C. Split the room: each group gets its own demo while the other does its written drill, then swap.
    - D. Send the Stage 2 kids home with worksheets.

38. A child still forgets the small-friend take-aways after nine days. What do you do?
    - A. Keep drilling the take-aways until they stick.
    - B. Move on, since they'll pick it up in Stage 3.
    - C. Give a written list of the formulas to memorize.
    - D. Go back to the small-friend adds, repeat that drill until it passes three days running, then come forward.

39. A parent of an online student asks if she can leave the room during the video lesson. What do you say?
    - A. "Sure, I'll keep an eye on him."
    - B. "Please stay in the room with the camera on for the whole lesson."
    - C. "Yes, as long as he has his soroban."
    - D. "Yes, but check in halfway through."

40. Which of these may you promise the parent of a child starting Stage 1?
    - A. "By the end of the first stage, your child will read any number off the beads in a glance, and add and take away when the beads are free."
    - B. "Her school grades will go up within two weeks."
    - C. "His IQ will rise by the end of the year."
    - D. "This will improve her memory and attention."
