# L19 · Common mistakes, and what to say

How to read a mistake from the size of the miss, and the short lines that get a child unstuck without ever giving the answer.

**Week** 6 · **Video** <!-- runtime:start -->about 6 min<!-- runtime:end --> · **Lesson time** 75 min · **Mirrors** Playbook chapter 8 ("What to say when they get stuck") and the trainer's stuck help and parent lines (`js/coach.js`)

## Video script

[HUNTER: talking head at the kitchen table. Hold up a worksheet with one wrong answer circled when you get to "read the miss".]

This is the lesson you'll come back to. Every child gets stuck. What you say in the next five seconds decides whether they learn something or wait for you.

The full list of lines is in chapter 8 of the Playbook. Read it once this week. Here's how to use it as a teacher.

### Never the answer

You know this one. The decision is the skill. Every time you give the answer, you take the rep away.

So the first rule of every stuck moment is the same. Ask a question. Wait. Hands in your lap.

### The three lines

If you remember nothing else, remember three lines. Between them, they cover almost every moment at the beads.

[on screen: "A bead only counts when it touches the beam." · "Free beads, or friend?" · "Let it go. Next one."]

"A bead only counts when it touches the beam." For any reading or setting trouble.

"Free beads, or friend?" For any add or take-away trouble, at every stage.

"Let it go. Next one." For any flash round or mental sum that falls apart.

### Read the miss

Here's a skill most parents never learn, and you will. A wrong answer tells you what went wrong, if you look at the size of the miss.

[on screen: Off by 5 → heaven bead · 10 too few → lost carry · 10 too many → lost borrow · off by a whole line → skipped line]

Off by exactly five? One heaven bead in the wrong place. Usually a small-friend move done halfway: the earth beads moved, the heaven bead didn't. Ask "Free beads, or friend?" on that rod.

Ten too few? A friend was taken away, but the carry never happened. Say "Take away the friend, then carry one to the left."

Ten too many? The friend was added, but the borrow never happened. Say "Add the friend, then borrow one from the left."

Off by a whole line? The child skipped a line on the page. Say "Finger under the line you're on."

Read the miss first. Then say one line. One.

### When they count beads

Reading is slow because the child is counting, not seeing. Go back to the peek. Shorten it until counting is impossible. Ask "What does seven look like?" The answer isn't a number. It's a picture: heaven plus two.

### When the school method creeps in

The eyes jump to the ones column. Say "left to right." Nothing else. Every time.

### When they forget a formula

Normal in the first week of a new group. Ask "What's the friend of three?" If it lasts past a week, go back one lesson, and repeat that drill until it passes three days running.

### When they rush

Seven out of ten, fast. Say "slow is fine," flat, as a fact. Time nothing for a week.

### When they're stuck at a boss

"The boss is just the lessons, in a row, with a clock." Find the part that failed, go back to its lessons for three days, try again.

### When the picture collapses

"Back to the beads, then back to your eyes."

### When there are tears

Stop for the day, warmly. "We're done for today. Good work. Same time tomorrow." Put the soroban away without a sigh. Tomorrow, a shorter session. A child who links the beads with pressure won't build a picture of them.

### When they want to quit

Usually in Stage 3, when carrying is new. Shorten the session. Drop to the daily quest alone. Don't bargain. Don't give a speech. It passes when the carries go automatic.

### In a class

A class adds three problems a kitchen table doesn't have.

The shouter. One child calls out answers, and the others stop thinking. Fix it with whiteboards or hands up, and say the rule once: "Answers at the end, on your board."

The copier. A child watches the next child's beads. Seat kids so they can't see each other's soroban during written drills.

The hovering parent. A parent at the back of the room says the answer under their breath. Give parents the same rule you follow. Hands in your lap, no answers. Hand them the one line for the stage.

### The praise that works

"You said the formula before you moved." "You went left to right without me saying it." "You let that round go and took the next one."

Each one names a thing the child did and can do again. That's the difference between a child who tries the next hard thing and one who protects a label.

### When you're the one who is stuck

A child asks something you can't answer. Say so. Press "show me" in the trainer together, and watch the beads do it. You were never supposed to know every answer. You were supposed to sit there.

## Teach-back

Role-play five stuck moments with a partner playing the child: counting beads, a lost carry, rushing, tears, and "I want to quit." Your partner picks the order and doesn't tell you. Record it. Score yourself: one point for each moment where you said one short line and no answer. Five of five, or do it again.

## Check: five questions

1. Write the three stuck lines and what each one is for.
2. Which size of miss points to a heaven bead in the wrong place, and what do you ask?

4. A child starts crying in the middle of a drill. What do you do and say?
5. In a class, one child keeps shouting answers. What do you change?
