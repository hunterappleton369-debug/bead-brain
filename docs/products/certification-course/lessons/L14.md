# L14 · The Bead Score and the kyu ladder

The one number that measures the mental picture, how to give the test, and how the rungs line up with the Japanese exam grades.

**Week** 4 · **Video** <!-- runtime:start -->about 6 min<!-- runtime:end --> · **Lesson time** 75 min · **Mirrors** Stage 5 lesson "Your first Bead Score", the Bead Score and Times ladders (`js/ladder.js`) and Playbook chapter 7

## Video script

[HUNTER: talking head. Show the 20 rungs on screen as a ladder graphic, then the age chart.]

Every parent asks the same question in different words. "How is my child doing, really?"

Not "did they pass the lesson." Where do they stand? The Bead Score is the answer.

### What it is

The Bead Score is a ladder with twenty rungs. It's taken with no soroban, no paper and no help. It measures one thing: the picture in the child's head.

The bottom rung is adding two small numbers. The top rung is fifteen three-digit numbers flashed at a fifth of a second each.

[on screen: Rung 1 → Rung 20]

The test starts at the bottom and climbs. Each rung has four problems. Three right clears it. The test stops at the first rung that fails. The score is the top rung cleared.

### The rungs that matter most

Rung 6 is three two-digit numbers, plus and minus, in the head. That's where most adults top out.

Rung 7 is the first flash rung. Five one-digit numbers at one second each. That's about 18th kyu on the Japanese flash ladder.

Rung 11 is five two-digit numbers flashed at one second. About 7th kyu.

Rung 12 is five three-digit numbers, plus and minus. That's the format of the Japanese 3rd-kyu mental exam.

Rung 15 is ten three-digit numbers, plus and minus. That's the format of the 1st-kyu mental exam, the top grade below dan.

[on screen: 6 adults · 7 ≈ 18th kyu flash · 11 ≈ 7th kyu · 12 = 3rd-kyu format · 15 = 1st-kyu format]

### The Japanese ladders

Here's what those grades are. There are three ladders in Japan, and the trainer is set against all of them.

The bead exams run from 10th kyu up to 1st kyu. 10th kyu is two-digit numbers, five lines. 1st kyu is ten-digit numbers, ten lines, plus sixty multiply and divide problems. Dan grades triple the 1st-kyu volume. Club kids in Japan usually reach 1st kyu around sixth grade.

The mental exams, called anzan, run 4th kyu to 1st. 4th kyu is two digits, five lines. 1st kyu is three digits, ten lines: twenty problems in twelve minutes, plus multiply and divide.

The flash anzan ladder runs from 20th kyu up to 20th dan. Ten questions per grade. Seven right to pass. 20th kyu is three one-digit numbers in five seconds. 10th dan is fifteen three-digit numbers in three seconds.

[on screen: Flash anzan · 20th kyu: three 1-digit in 5 s · 12th kyu: ten 2-digit in 10 s · 1st kyu: fifteen 2-digit in 13 s · 2nd dan: four 3-digit in 4 s · 5th dan: ten 3-digit in 7 s · 10th dan: fifteen 3-digit in 3 s]

### Say "about," always

We are calibrated to those ladders. We are not those exams, and we don't award their grades. So you never say "she's 7th kyu." You say "she cleared rung 11. That's about 7th kyu on the Japanese flash ladder."

That one word keeps you honest, and parents trust it more.

### How to give it

The first Bead Score comes at the end of Stage 5. Before that, the lessons are the measure.

No soroban. No paper. No help. Sit beside the child and say nothing until it's over. A test takes about ten minutes. The early rungs feel easy. Then one feels like work. Then one doesn't go. That's the score.

The stop isn't a failure. It's the ladder doing its job, which is to find the edge of the picture.

### How often

Every two to four weeks, in place of the day's mental drill. Not more. The ladder doesn't move in a week, and a child tested every few days learns to dread it.

Never test tired. A cranky child at bedtime tests two rungs below where they live. If a score comes in lower than last time, that's what happened. A normal two weeks, then retake it.

Plateaus are normal. They last about two weeks and break overnight. Keep the daily drill the same size.

### The chart

Each score goes on a chart in the trainer. The child's own line climbs test by test. Behind it are shaded bands for where kids of each age usually sit.

Be careful here. Those bands are estimates, built from what each school grade expects. They are not a measured sample of kids. The child's own line is the honest number. Say it that way.

### The Times ladder

Stage 6 opens a second ladder for multiply and divide. Ten rungs, from times tables to five, up to three-digit times three-digit in the head.

### What the parent does with the number

Put it where the child can see it. The fridge works. Say it to the grandparents: "She's on rung 8. That's five two-digit numbers added in her head. Try it."

And film a test every few months. The clip is the proof. The number is the score.

## Teach-back

Give a partner (adult or child) the first six rungs from section A to F, read aloud, with no soroban and no paper. Stop at the first rung they miss. Then explain their score to them in three sentences, using "about" correctly and naming one thing that would move them up a rung.

4. How often should a child take the Bead Score, and when should you not test?
