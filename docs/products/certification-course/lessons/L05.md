# L05 · Small friends, adding

The first real technique: when you can't add a number directly, add five and take away its friend.

**Week** 2 · **Video** <!-- runtime:start -->about 6 min<!-- runtime:end --> · **Lesson time** 90 min · **Mirrors** Stage 2 (Explorer) lessons "+1 and +4: friends 1 and 4", "+2 and +3: friends 2 and 3" and "Small-friend adds in 2-digit numbers"

## Video script

[HUNTER: close-up on one rod. Use an orange marker or sticker for small-friend moves on screen. Orange is the small-friend color in the trainer.]

Last lesson ended on a question. What if the beads aren't free?

Set 4 on one rod. Four earth beads at the beam. Now add 1. Look for a free earth bead. There isn't one. All four are up.

This is the moment every child hits in Stage 2. And the answer is a friend.

### Small friends make five

Two numbers that make five are small friends. One and four. Two and three.

[on screen: 1 + 4 = 5 · 2 + 3 = 5]

Here's the move. When you can't add a number directly, add five and take away its friend.

Back to our rod. It shows 4. We want to add 1. The friend of 1 is 4. So bring the heaven bead down. That's plus five. Then push four earth beads away. That's minus four. The rod shows 5.

Plus one is plus five, take away four. Say it with me. "Plus one: add five, take away four."

[on screen: +1 = +5 −4]

### All four adds

There are four small-friend adds, one for each number from one to four.

Plus one is plus five, take away four. Plus two is plus five, take away three. Plus three is plus five, take away two. Plus four is plus five, take away one.

[on screen: +1 = +5 −4 · +2 = +5 −3 · +3 = +5 −2 · +4 = +5 −1]

Try three. The rod shows 3. Add 4. Free earth beads? Only one. We need four. So friend. Heaven bead down, plus five. Take away one, the friend of four. The rod shows 7.

The rod shows 1. Add 4. Three earth beads are free. We need four. Friend again. Plus five, take away one. The rod shows 5.

The rod shows 4. Add 3. No earth bead is free. Plus five, take away two. The rod shows 7.

### When a small friend shows up

Look at what those rods had in common. The heaven bead was up. And there weren't enough earth beads free.

That's the only time a small-friend add happens. If the heaven bead is already down and the number won't fit, that's a different move. It's a big friend, and it comes in Lesson 7. For now, the drills never ask for it. The trainer makes sure of that.

### Teach them in pairs

The trainer teaches these in two pairs. Plus one and plus four first, because they are friends of each other. Then plus two and plus three.

The pairing helps. A child learning plus one is also seeing plus four, from the other side. Same two numbers, same picture.

### Free beads, or friend?

Now here's the most important thing in this lesson. It's not the move. It's the decision.

The drills in this course mix in a few problems with free beads, and so does the Explorer boss. On purpose. The child has to stop and ask, every time, "Free beads, or friend?"

[on screen: "Free beads, or friend?"]

That's the question you'll ask a child more than any other, for years. When they freeze, you don't say "bring the five down." You ask, "Free beads, or friend?" And then you wait. The wait is where the learning is.

### Two digits

Inside a two-digit problem, the friend shows up on one rod and not the other. Thirteen plus twelve. Tens first: one plus one. Free beads. Ones: three plus two. Only one earth bead free, so friend. Plus five, take away three. The answer is 25.

Forty-two plus eleven. Tens: four plus one. No free bead. Friend. Plus five, take away four. Ones: two plus one. Free beads. The answer is 53.

On every rod of every problem, the same question.

### Saying the line

Have the child say the line out loud while their hand moves. "Plus three: add five, take away two." The words run a half step ahead of the fingers and tell them where to go.

In the first week of a new move, they say it out loud. In the second week, they whisper it. By the third, it's quiet. The hand just moves. That's what automatic means.

### Slips to watch

The child brings the five down and forgets to take the friend away. The answer comes out too big by the friend. Ask, "What's the friend of three?"

Or the child takes away the number instead of its friend. Plus three, take away three. The rod is now off. Same question. "What's the friend of three?"

Next lesson: the same four moves, run backwards.

## Teach-back

Teach "+1 and +4" to a partner in ten minutes. Use section A. Rules for you: say the line once as a model, then only ever ask "Free beads, or friend?" and "What's the friend of ___?" Do not touch the beads. Record it. Count how long you waited after each question. Most new instructors wait under two seconds. Aim for five.

## Check: five questions

1. Name the two pairs of small friends.
2. Write all four small-friend adds as formulas.
3. The rod shows 2. Add 4. Free beads or friend? Give the move and the answer.
4. When can a small-friend add happen? Describe the rod.
5. A child doing 3 + 4 gets 8. What most likely happened, and what do you ask?
