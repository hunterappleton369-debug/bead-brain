# L15 · Multiplication on the beads

Times tables plus adding: every small product is two digits, and its right-hand digit lands in the column of its place value.

## Video script

[HUNTER: over the shoulder, problem written on a card beside the soroban, answer built on the beads.]

Multiplication on a soroban is two things you already have. Times tables. And adding. Your hands already add fast. So the only new skill is knowing where each small product lands.

### First, the facts

Before a child multiplies on the beads, the times tables up to nine times nine must be instant. Not "I can work it out." Instant.

So the first lesson of Stage 6 is a test. Twenty facts, under a minute, almost none wrong. If a child misses, that lesson goes into the daily quest until they pass it three days in a row.

[on screen: 20 facts · under 60 s · 95%]

The parent line for this stage: "Times tables five minutes a day for two weeks before the first multiply lesson pays off."

### The one rule

Write every small product as two digits. Three times seven is 21. Two times four is 08, zero eight. Always two digits.

The right-hand digit of each product lands in the column of its place value. That's the whole rule.

[on screen: every product = 2 digits · right digit lands on its place]

### Two digits times one

Thirty-four times seven. The problem stays on the card. Only the answer goes on the beads.

Start with the left digit. Three times seven is 21. That 3 is in the tens. So this product is 21 tens. Its right digit, the 1, lands on the tens rod. The 2 lands on the hundreds. Add 21 there.

Next, four times seven is 28. The 4 is in the ones. So the 8 lands on the ones rod and the 2 on the tens. Add 28 there.

Read the beads. 238.

[on screen: 34 × 7 → 21 (hundreds-tens) + 28 (tens-ones) = 238]

Notice the adds. Adding 28 onto 210 uses the same moves the child has owned since Stage 4. A carry, maybe a friend. Nothing new.

Eighty-six times seven. Eight times seven, 56, on hundreds and tens. Six times seven, 42, on tens and ones. 602.

### Three digits times one

Same rule, one more digit. Three hundred eighty-six times seven.

Three times seven, 21, lands on thousands and hundreds. Eight times seven, 56, on hundreds and tens. Six times seven, 42, on tens and ones. The answer builds itself: 2,702.

Each product sits one rod to the right of the last. Left to right, again.

### Two digits times two

Thirty-four times twenty-seven has four small products.

Take the 3 first. It's in the tens. Three times two, and the 2 is also tens. Tens times tens lands on the hundreds. So 06 goes on thousands and hundreds. Three times seven, 21, goes one rod right: hundreds and tens.

Now the 4. Four times two, 08, on hundreds and tens. Four times seven, 28, on tens and ones.

The answer is 918.

[on screen: 34 × 27 → 06 · 21 · 08 · 28 → 918]

The pattern: each digit of the first number works through every digit of the second. Each step moves one rod to the right.

Three digits times two digits is six products. Three hundred eighty-six times forty-seven comes out to 18,142. Keep a steady rhythm and the landing spots look after themselves.

### Paper for the problem, beads for the answer

Japanese teachers keep both numbers off the beads, on paper, and build only the answer. That's the method experts use. And it's the exact method a child uses later, in the head.

### Mental: two digits times one

Now the same thing on the mental beads. Forty-seven times six. Four times six is 24, on hundreds and tens. Picture 240. Seven times six is 42. Add it. Say 282.

Keep the product on the beads, not in words. If the child says "two hundred forty" in their head, the picture is slipping into words.

### Keep the old picture warm

Multiplication weeks can let the add-and-subtract picture fade. The last lesson of Stage 6 is just five lines of two digits in the head, mixed. Keep a little of it every day.

### The Wizard boss

[on screen: Wizard Boss · Times tables: 20 in 60 s, 90% · 3-digit × 1-digit: 6 problems, 4 min · 2-digit × 2-digit: 5 problems, 5 min · Mental 2 × 1: 5 problems, 150 s]

Four parts. Times tables. Three digits by one. Two by two. And mental two by one.

Wizard takes about six to eight weeks. It also opens the Times ladder, the second ladder on the chart. Ten rungs, from tables to five, up to three digits times three in the head.

### What you need for this stage

You need to be one stage ahead of the kids you teach. So do the drills below until 2-digit by 2-digit feels calm. You don't need it fast.

## Teach-back

Teach "2-digit × 1-digit" to a partner using two problems from section A. Rules: they must say each small product as two digits ("twenty-one", "zero eight") and point to the rod where its right digit lands before they add it. Record it. Note any product they placed one rod off, and what you said.

## Check: five questions

1. What must be true before a child starts multiplying on the beads?
2. State the landing rule in one sentence.
3. 34 × 7: give the two small products and where each lands.
4. 34 × 27: list the four small products in order.
5. Why do we keep the problem on paper and only the answer on the beads?
