# L16 · Division on the beads

Multiplication run backwards: guess a digit, multiply, take away, repeat. Everything the child needs is already in their hands.

**Week** 5 · **Video** <!-- runtime:start -->about 5 min<!-- runtime:end --> · **Lesson time** 105 min · **Mirrors** Stage 7 (Champion) lessons "Divide 2-digit by 1-digit", "Divide 3-digit by 1-digit", "Remainders", "Divide by 2-digit numbers", "Mental: divide by 1-digit", "Mental: three 3-digit numbers", "Flash: 2-digit numbers" and the Champion Boss

Division is the last of the four operations. And it's multiplication run backwards. Guess a digit. Multiply. Take away. Repeat.

That's all of it. Guess, multiply, take away.

[on screen: Guess → Multiply → Take away → Repeat]

### Setting it up

Set the number you're dividing, the dividend, on the right side of the soroban. Leave a gap of empty rods. The answer, the quotient, gets built on the rods to the left.

### Two digits by one

Eighty-four divided by seven. Look at the first digit, 8. How many sevens fit in 8? One. Put 1 in the quotient. Seven times one is 7. Take 70 away from the beads. Fourteen is left.

How many sevens in 14? Two. Put 2 in the quotient. Seven times two is 14. Take it away. The beads read zero. The quotient reads 12.

[on screen: 84 ÷ 7 = 12]

### Three digits by one

Nine hundred fifty-two divided by seven. How many sevens in 9? One. Quotient 1. Take away 700. Two hundred fifty-two is left.

Now look at 25. How many sevens? Three. Quotient 13. Three times seven is 21. Take away 210. Forty-two is left.

How many sevens in 42? Six. Quotient 136. Take away 42. Zero.

[on screen: 952 ÷ 7 = 136]

Sometimes the first digit is too small. Four hundred twenty-six divided by six. Six doesn't fit in 4. So start with the first two digits, 42. Seven sixes. Then 6 divided by 6 is one. The answer is 71.

### Remainders

When nothing more fits, what's left on the beads is the remainder. Nine hundred fifty divided by seven. One hundred thirty-five sevens, and five is left over. 135, remainder 5.

### Dividing by two digits

Now the hard one. Two thousand seven hundred fifty-two divided by thirty-two.

Estimate with the first digits. Twenty-seven divided by three says nine. Try nine. Thirty-two times nine is 288. That's bigger than 275. Too big.

That's normal. It's not a mistake. Lower the guess by one. Try eight. Thirty-two times eight is 256. Take it away from 275. Nineteen left. Bring the next digit in: 192.

How many thirty-twos in 192? Try six. Thirty-two times six is 192. Take it away. Zero. The answer is 86.

[on screen: 2,752 ÷ 32 = 86 · first guess 9 is too big, so try 8]

On the beads, if you've already taken away and the guess was too big, you'll see it: you can't take away. Take one off the quotient, add the divisor back, and carry on.

Tell kids this before they start. A guess that's too big is part of division. The child who expects it doesn't panic when it happens.

### Where kids slip

The common slip is the quotient digit on the wrong rod. The child puts it one rod too far left, and the answer comes out ten times too big. Before each guess, have them point to the rod where the digit goes. Point, then place.

The other slip is taking the product away from the wrong rods. Same fix. Point to where the product lands, then take it away. It's the landing rule from Lesson 15, run backwards.

### Mental division

Same steps on the mental beads. Ninety-one divided by seven. Seven goes into nine once, two left. Twenty-one divided by seven is three. Say 13.

### The rest of Stage 7

Champion has two more mental lessons. Three three-digit numbers added in the head, which means three rods in the picture. That's the edge of what most people can hold. Slow is fine. Correct is the target.

And two-digit flash. Eight numbers at 0.7 seconds. The numbers go by faster than you can say them. That's the point. Words are too slow. The picture isn't.

### The Champion boss

[on screen: Champion Boss · 3-digit ÷ 1-digit with remainders: 6 problems, 4 min · 4-digit ÷ 2-digit: 5 problems, 5 min · Mental ÷: 5 problems, 150 s · Flash 2-digit: 8 numbers at 0.7 s, 5 rounds]

Four parts. Division with remainders. Division by two digits. Mental division. And two-digit flash.

Champion takes about six to eight weeks. A child who passes it can add, take away, multiply and divide in their head. That's more than most adults can do.

### The parent line

"Division is slow at first. Accuracy, then speed."

Say it to the parent at the start of the stage. Then say it again in week three, when they ask why it's still slow.

## Teach-back

Teach "Divide by 2-digit numbers" to a partner using one problem from section D. Pick one where the first guess is too big, so they meet it. Your only lines are "Guess", "Multiply", "Take away", and "Too big? Lower it by one." Record it and note how they reacted to the first too-big guess.
