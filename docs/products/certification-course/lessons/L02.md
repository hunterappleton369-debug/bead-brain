# L02 · The soroban, the hand, reading and setting

How the tool works, how the hand moves, and how a child learns to see a number on the beads in under three seconds.

**Week** 1 · **Video** <!-- runtime:start -->about 5 min<!-- runtime:end --> · **Lesson time** 90 min · **Mirrors** Stage 1 (Sprout) lessons "Hold it, clear it, move it", "Read any number", "Read hundreds in a glance", "Set numbers fast" and "Eyes closed"

## Video script

[HUNTER: record over the shoulder, soroban flat on the table, hands in frame. A close-up camera on the beads helps.]

Pick up your soroban. Lay it flat in front of you, beam running left to right.

[on screen: heaven bead · beam · earth beads]

Above the beam, each rod has one bead. It is worth five. We call it the heaven bead. Below the beam, each rod has four beads. Each one is worth one. We call them earth beads.

Now the one rule that runs everything. A bead counts when it touches the beam. A bead that sits away from the beam is worth nothing.

[on screen: "A bead only counts when it touches the beam."]

Say that line out loud. You will say it to kids a thousand times. It fixes almost every reading mistake.

### Clearing

Before every problem, the soroban shows zero. Tilt the top edge up so the beads slide away from the beam. Lay it flat. Then run your index finger along the top of the beam to push the heaven beads up. Every rod now reads zero.

Do it three times now. Fast and quiet is the goal.

### The hand

Use your right hand. Your left hand holds the frame still.

Your thumb pushes earth beads up to the beam. Your index finger does everything else. It pulls earth beads down. It moves the heaven bead, up and down.

That split matters. A child who uses one finger for everything is slow, and slow hands make a weak picture later. Watch the thumb in the first week. Fix it kindly, every time.

### The shapes

Every digit has a shape. One earth bead up is 1. Four up is 4. The heaven bead down, alone, is 5. Heaven plus two is 7. Heaven plus four is 9.

[on screen: the digits 0 to 9 as bead shapes]

The goal of the first week is for a child to know these shapes the way they know letters. Not count them. Know them.

So when you teach it, name the shape. "Seven is heaven plus two." "Four is four up, heaven off." Children copy the words you use. Use shape words.

### Reading

Each rod is a place. The rod you choose for ones is the ones. The next one left is the tens. Then the hundreds. Small dots on the beam mark every third rod, so you can find your ones rod fast.

To read, see each rod's shape and say the whole number. The common slip is a zero in the middle. Three hundred five has an empty tens rod. An empty rod still holds a place.

[on screen: 305, tens rod empty]

Aim for under three seconds a number. Slower than that, and the child is counting beads one by one. You will see it. Their lips move. Their eyes jump from bead to bead.

The fix is the peek. Set a number. Show it for two seconds. Cover it with your hand. Ask, "What was it?" A child cannot count in two seconds, so they have to see the shape. The trainer has a peek mode that does the same.

### Setting

Setting runs the other way. Hear a number, then set it from the left. Hundreds first, then tens, then ones.

Why from the left? Because a child will add from the left later. Every big move in this method runs left to right. The habit starts here, on day one, with setting.

### Eyes closed

Here is the last drill of Stage 1, and it plants the seed of everything. Set a one-digit number. Let the child look for about one second. Then they close their eyes and say it.

Can they still see it? That faint picture is the start of the mental abacus. It is tiny now, one rod. Later it holds three or four.

### How to run it with a child

Say every number out loud with the child. Keep it short. Stop while it is still fun. Ten minutes is a full lesson at this stage.

And keep your hands in your lap. If you set the beads for them, you take the rep away.

That is Stage 1, the first half. In the next lesson, we start moving numbers. See you there.

## Teach-back

Teach reading to someone who has never seen a soroban: a partner, a friend, or a child you know. Use only three things: the shape words ("seven is heaven plus two"), the line "a bead only counts when it touches the beam", and the two-second peek. Ten minutes. Then write down one thing they got wrong and the exact words you used to fix it.

## Check: five questions

1. What is the heaven bead worth, and what are the earth beads worth?
2. Which finger pushes earth beads up? Which finger moves the heaven bead?
3. A child takes six seconds to read 47. What is going on, and which drill fixes it?
4. Why do we set numbers from the left?
5. The tens rod shows the heaven bead and three earth beads at the beam. The ones rod shows two earth beads. What number is it?
