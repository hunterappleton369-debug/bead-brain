# L03 · Direct moves: when the beads are free

The ground every one of the 34 moves stands on: adding when the beads are free, and taking away when the beads are there.

**Week** 1 · **Video** <!-- runtime:start -->about 5 min<!-- runtime:end --> · **Lesson time** 75 min · **Mirrors** Stage 1 lessons "Add when the beads are free" and "Take away when the beads are there"

## Video script

[HUNTER: over the shoulder, one rod in close-up for the single-rod examples.]

Today we start moving numbers. And we start with the easiest kind of move, the one the whole method stands on.

To add on a soroban, you push more beads to the beam. To take away, you push beads off it. That's it. The only question is whether the beads you need are free.

[on screen: "Are the beads free?"]

### Adding

Set 3 on one rod. Three earth beads at the beam. Now add 1. Is there a free earth bead? Yes, one is still down. Thumb pushes it up. The rod shows 4.

Clear. Set 1. Add 5. Is the heaven bead free? Yes, it is up, away from the beam. Index finger brings it down. The rod shows 6.

Clear. Set 6. That's the heaven bead plus one. Add 3. Are three earth beads free? There are three left below. Push all three up at once. The rod shows 9.

Notice "all three at once." Not one, two, three. The child moves the number 3 as one shape, one push. That habit matters. A child who adds by counting beads is still counting on fingers, just with wood.

### Taking away

Set 8. Take away 3. Are three earth beads on the beam? Yes. Index finger pulls them down, together. The rod shows 5.

Set 7. Take away 5. Is the heaven bead on the beam? Yes. Push it up. The rod shows 2.

### How you know a bead is free

Here is the check you run in your head while the child works. Say the rod shows 2 and the child needs to add 3. Two earth beads are up. Two are free. They need three. So the beads are not free. That is not a direct move.

For adding a number under five, look at the earth beads that are still down. Are there enough? For adding five, is the heaven bead up? For adding six to nine, you need both: the heaven bead free and enough earth beads free.

Taking away runs the same check the other way. Are the beads you want to remove touching the beam?

You need this check so you never hand a child a problem they cannot do yet. In Stage 1, every problem must be direct. The trainer builds its problems that way. It runs each one on a model of the soroban and throws out any that needs a move the child has not learned. If you ever write your own problems, run each line on your own beads first.

### Why one rod first

We start on one rod because one rod has no carrying and no place value to juggle. The child learns one thing: free beads move. And they learn to ask the question.

That question, "are the beads free?", is the first of four questions. Every rod of every problem, for years, starts with it. Today the answer is always yes. Soon it will be no. Then the friends arrive.

### Two slips to watch

The first is the bounce. The child pushes a bead up, lets go too soon, and it slides back down. Now the rod reads wrong and they don't know why. Teach a firm push and a clean lift of the finger.

The second is the wrong finger. Thumb up, index down, index for the heaven bead. Fix it every time, with no fuss. "Thumb for up." Three words, then move on. In a week the hand does it without thinking.

### What you say

When a child moves the wrong beads, do not move them back for her. Ask, "What does the rod show now?" Then, "What did you want it to show?" Let her fix it.

When she gets it right, name what she did. "You moved all three at once." Praise the move, not the answer.

### Your homework

Below are twenty single-rod moves. Do every one on your soroban. Say "free beads" out loud before each move. It feels silly. It builds the habit you will pass on.

## Teach-back

Pick five problems from section A. Teach them to a partner using only questions. You may say "Are the beads free?", "What does the rod show now?" and "What did you want it to show?" You may not touch the beads or say an answer. Record it, then count how many times you broke the rule.

## Check: five questions

1. In one sentence, what is a direct add?
2. The rod shows 2. Can you add 3 directly? Why or why not?
3. The rod shows 6. Can you add 3 directly?
4. Why should a child move three beads in one push, not one at a time?
5. A child moves the wrong beads. What do you say first?
