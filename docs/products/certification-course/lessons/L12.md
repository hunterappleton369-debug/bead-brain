# L12 · Running a 15-minute session and a 45-minute class

The daily home routine the trainer prints, the week it fits into, and a 45-minute group class built from the same parts.

**Week** 4 · **Video** <!-- runtime:start -->about 6 min<!-- runtime:end --> · **Lesson time** 90 min · **Mirrors** the trainer's daily routine and weekly rhythm, Stage 5 lessons "Fast hands" and "Read ahead", and the class plans in the Instructor Launch Kit

## Video script

[HUNTER: kitchen table for the first half, then a table with four chairs for the class half.]

Every family you teach will ask the same question. "How much should we practice?" Here's the answer, and the reason.

### Fifteen minutes, every day

Short and daily beats long and weekly. Every proven program says it. Even the ones that meet once a week send kids home with fifteen to twenty minutes a day.

A child's picture of the beads is built in reps, spread out. Two hours on a Saturday is one long rep. Fifteen minutes, six days a week, is six. And a fifteen-minute session ends before the child is tired. Tired reps build a weak picture.

### The fifteen minutes

The trainer prints the routine. Follow it.

[on screen: Warm-up 2 min · Lesson or drill 8–10 min · Mental 5 min · Log]

Warm-up, two minutes. Reading or setting, something easy that wins.

Lesson or drill, eight to ten minutes. The day's lesson, or the drill the child is repeating until it passes.

Mental, five minutes. Soroban away, eyes closed, a few rounds.

Then log it. The trainer logs it for you. On paper, a tick on the calendar is enough.

### The week

[on screen: Mon–Thu lesson · Fri quest only · Sat boss · Sun off]

Monday to Thursday, a lesson. Friday, the daily quest alone: three short drills, with a bonus for finishing all three. Saturday, the boss, if the child is ready. Sunday, off.

And a Bead Score test every two to four weeks, in place of the day's mental drill. We cover that in Lesson 14.

### The chair

The parent's whole job is a chair next to the child. Beside, not across. Same spot, same time, every day. The kitchen table after breakfast works for a lot of families.

Tell the parents this, early. They don't need to know any math. They need to sit there, ask "free beads, or friend?", and keep their hands in their lap.

### The forty-five-minute class

Now a class. Four to eight kids, all at the same stage. Same parts as the home session, stretched, with more voices.

[on screen: 0–5 warm-up · 5–10 dictation · 10–25 lesson · 25–35 written · 35–41 flash or mental · 41–45 log and homework]

Minutes zero to five. Warm-up. The peek game: you set a number on a big soroban or the screen, show it for two seconds, hide it. Everyone writes or says it.

Five to ten. Dictation. You read, they work it on the beads. Short problems, one number every two seconds.

Ten to twenty-five. The new lesson. Demo it once on the big soroban or the screen abacus. Then the whole group says the line together. "Plus three: add five, take away two." Then pairs: one works, one asks "free beads, or friend?"

Twenty-five to thirty-five. Written drill. A printed sheet from the trainer, matched to the lesson. Accuracy first. Time it only if the group is at nine of ten.

Thirty-five to forty-one. Flash or mental. You read. Everyone keeps the total. Hands up at the end, or answers on a whiteboard so nobody shouts.

Forty-one to forty-five. Log, and the homework. The homework is always the same: the fifteen minutes at home, in the trainer, every day until next class.

The Launch Kit has eight weeks of these plans written out for Sprout and Explorer. You don't have to build them. Use them.

### The class does not replace the home minutes

Say this to every parent. The class is where the child learns the move. The fifteen minutes at home is where the move becomes automatic. A child who comes once a week and never practices will crawl. A child who comes once a week and does the daily fifteen will climb.

### Mixed levels

Sooner or later, you'll have kids at two stages in one room. Split them. Each group gets its own demo while the other does its written drill. Then swap. Never teach a move to a child who doesn't own the group before it.

### Online

The screen abacus and the flash trainer work over any video call. Ask the child to hold their soroban up to the camera at the end of each problem, so you can see the beads. Everything else runs the same.

### Your homework

Section A to C below is a full fifteen-minute session pack for a Stage 2 child, ready to run. Run it with a child or a partner, with a timer, and see where the minutes go.

Sections D and E are for you. Ten-line, three-digit problems. Time yourself. You don't need to be fast. You need to be one stage ahead of the kids you teach.
