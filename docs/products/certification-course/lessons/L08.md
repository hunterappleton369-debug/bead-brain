# L08 · Big friends: borrowing, and the Apprentice boss

Taking away a number that isn't there: add the big friend, then borrow one from the left. Then borrow chains, mixed problems and the first mental crossing of ten.

### The move

To take away a number that isn't there, add the big friend on this rod, then borrow one from the rod on the left.

Thirteen take away nine. Add the big friend of nine, which is one. Push one earth bead up on the ones rod. Now borrow one from the tens: pull that earth bead down. The beads show 4.

"Minus nine: add one, borrow one."

### Which goes first

For borrows, add the friend first, then borrow. Work the rod you are on first, then the rod to its left. For adding: take away the friend, then carry. For taking away: add the friend, then borrow. One rule for both.

The trainer says it in the same order: "Minus 8: add 2, borrow 1 from the left."

Both orders land on the same beads. What matters is that a child does it the same way every time. Teach this order, and never switch it on a child mid-stage.

### Borrow chains

Sometimes the rod you borrow from is empty. One hundred take away one. The ones rod is empty, so add nine, then borrow one from the tens. The tens rod is empty too, so it does the same: add nine, then borrow one from the hundreds. Ninety-nine. Each borrow is just another minus one, with the same rules.

Two hundred five take away seven. Add three, the friend of seven, on the ones. Then borrow. The tens rod is empty, so it adds nine and borrows from the hundreds. One hundred ninety-eight.

[on screen: 205 − 7 → 198]

And a borrow can need a small friend, the same way a carry can. Say the tens rod shows 5 and you borrow one. Minus one on a rod showing five. No earth bead on the beam. Small friend: minus five, add four. Same questions, every rod.

### Two slips to watch

The child adds the friend and forgets to borrow. The answer comes out ten too big. Ask, "Where did the ten come from?"

Or the child borrows and forgets the friend. The answer is too small by the friend. Ask, "What's the friend of nine?" Both slips leave a clue in the size of the miss. You'll learn to read those clues in Lesson 19.

### Mixed problems

Then the lesson that matters most in this stage. Five lines. Plus and minus, mixed. Every move so far. Small friends, carries and borrows, all in one problem. Nothing is new. It's just all of it at once.

### The mental rounds

Stage 3 ends with two short mental lessons. First, three small numbers that need small friends, in the head. Then four one-digit numbers, plus and minus, that cross ten.

So far the mental picture held one rod. Now it needs two: the ones rod, and the tens rod beside it for the carry. That's a real jump. Expect slower answers for a week.

Read each number once, on a steady beat. "Seven. Minus three. Eight. Five." The child says one thing at the end. The total.

### The Apprentice boss

[on screen: Apprentice Boss · Carry or borrow: 10 moves, 150 s, 9 right · 2-digit, 5 lines: 6 problems, 5 min, 5 right · Mental: 5 problems of four 1-digit numbers, 150 s, 4 right]

Part one: ten carry-or-borrow moves on the screen abacus, two and a half minutes, no hints. Nine right. Part two: six problems, two digits, five lines, five minutes. Five right. Part three: five mental problems, four one-digit numbers each. Four right.

### What to tell the parent

The parent line for Stage 3: "Carries feel backwards for a week. Insist on left to right every time."

And tell them about the quitting week before it comes. A parent who knows it's coming doesn't panic when it does.

### Where you are now

You now know 26 of the 34 moves. Eight small friends. Eighteen big friends. The last eight need both at once, and that's next lesson.
