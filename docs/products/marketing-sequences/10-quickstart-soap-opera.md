### Your 3-Night Quick-Start

Start tonight on the screen abacus: {{link_stage1}}. Sign in with the email you ordered with. When the box lands, do the same three nights again with the real beads.

**Every night:** 15 minutes. Sit beside, not across. Say each move out loud. Praise the move, not the answer. Stop while it is still fun.

**Night 1 · See it, move it, say it**
Push all the beads away from the beam. Thumb pushes beads up. Pointer finger pushes them down. Bottom beads are 1. The top bead is 5. A bead counts when it touches the beam. Set 1 to 9 out loud. Stuck? Ask, "Free beads, or friend?" Then wait.
Why tonight matters: the hands learn the beads. That is the first half of the picture.

**Night 2 · Bigger numbers**
Left rod is tens. Right rod is ones. Set 23, 47 and 85. Read each one back. Then cover the abacus and ask, "What did 47 look like?"
Why tonight matters: the picture starts to move inside.

**Night 3 · The first friend move**
Set 4. Add 1. No free bead! Bring the 5 down and send 4 away. Plus 1 is plus 5, take away 4. Not yet? That is fine. Stage 2 teaches it.
Why tonight matters: this is where numbers stop being memorized and start being moved.

Hi {{first_name}},

Here are the tactics. Seven of them. All small.

1. **Same time every day.** After dinner or right before bed. Set a timer for 15 minutes. Stop when it rings, even in the middle of a problem. Leave them wanting more.
2. **Sit beside, not across.** You are on the same team as the beads.
3. **Say the move, not the answer.** "Push one up." "Friend of 4." Never "it's seven."
4. **Praise the move, not the answer.** "You saw the five!" beats "You're so smart." The move is the thing they control.
5. **Stuck? Ask, "Free beads, or friend?"** Then wait ten seconds. Say nothing. The wait is where the brain works.
6. **End with a flash round.** Say three numbers. They set each one fast. Most kids ask for this part.
7. **Tonight is Night 3.** The first friend move. Set 4, add 1. If it does not land tonight, that is fine. Stage 2 teaches it.

The one mistake: rescuing. A parent sees the stuck face and gives the answer. The child learns that waiting gets rescued. Hold the ten seconds. The face changes.
