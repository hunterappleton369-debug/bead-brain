## Day 3 · Lesson 3

- **Subject:** The party trick
- **Preview:** Set 694. Cover it. Ask.
- **Link:** Open Lesson 3 ({{link_lesson_read3}})

Hi {{first_name}},

Today is Lesson 3: "Read hundreds in a glance."

This is the first party trick. Try it at dinner.

Set 694 on the beads. Let your child look for two seconds. Then cover the beads with your hand. Ask, "What was it?"

At first they'll count bead by bead. That's fine. By the end of this lesson the goal is a glance. The number goes in as a picture.

That picture is the whole game. Every stage after this one builds on it.

## Day 5 · Lessons 4 to 6

- **Subject:** The question that does the teaching
- **Preview:** Free beads, or friend?
- **Link:** Open today's lesson ({{link_stage1}})

Hi {{first_name}},

By now your child is setting numbers. Next come Lessons 5 and 6: "Add when the beads are free" and "Take away when the beads are there."

Adding is pushing beads toward the beam. Taking away is pushing them off. When the beads you need are sitting there free, you just move them.

This is where your one question starts. When your child stops and stares, ask:

**"Free beads, or friend?"**

Then wait. Don't touch the beads. Don't say the answer. The choice is the skill. Each time you make it for them, you take a rep away.

Friday is a quest day. No new lesson. Just two or three short drills. All three earns a bonus.

[Open today's lesson]({{link_stage1}})

## Day 7 · one week in

- **Subject:** One week. Here's what to look for.
- **Preview:** Small signs that it's working.
- **Link:** Print today's practice sheet ({{link_worksheet1}})

Hi {{first_name}},

One week down. Here is what to watch for. These signs are small, but they count.

- Your child reaches for the beads without being asked.
- They read a number out loud at the store or in a game.
- They correct you.

That last one is the big one. A child who corrects a parent's math has stopped believing they are "not a math kid."

**Next up: "Two columns, left to right."** Your child works the big column first, then the small one. School teaches the other way. Left to right is what makes mental math possible later, because it matches the way we say the number.

Your membership comes with practice sheets matched to the lesson. Print one for the fridge or the car.
