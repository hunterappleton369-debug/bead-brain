- **Preview:** What to look for, and why it counts.
- **Link, in the P.S.:** Open Stage 1 ({{link_stage1}})

Twelve days in. Time for your first proof.

Every Whole Brain family has a first-week video. It's short. It's shaky. It may be the best clip on your phone.

Here's how to make it. Set 694 on the beads. Let your child look for two seconds. Cover the beads with your hand. Ask, "What was it?" Film the whole thing.

Then hit reply and send it to me. I watch every one. I will never share it unless you say yes in writing.

While you film, watch for three small signs.

- Your child reaches for the beads without being asked.
- They read a number out loud at the store or in a game.
- They correct you.

That last one is the big one. A child who corrects a parent's math has stopped believing they are "not a math kid."

Keep the clip, even if it's slow. Slow is the point. In a year, you will want to see where it started.

## L4 · day 18

- **Subject:** The Sprout Boss, and how to cheer
- **Preview:** Passed or not yet, here's what to do.
- **Link, in the P.S.:** Try the Sprout Boss ({{link_boss1}})

Hi {{first_name}},

Stage 1 is built to take two to three weeks. So the Sprout Boss may be close.

It's the test at the end of Stage 1. Three short timed parts. No hints. Pass all three and your child earns the Sprout badge.

If your child passed, cheer the Whole Brain way.

- **Name the move.** "You read that number in a glance. I saw it."
- **Make it real.** Write "Sprout" on a card. Let your child draw the badge. Put it on the fridge.
- **Tell someone.** Call a grandparent. Let your child do the cover trick for them.

Don't cheer for being smart. Cheer for the work. 15 minutes a day, for weeks. That's what won.

Not passed yet? That's fine. Not every child passes on the first try. A failed boss is a map. It shows which part needs more work. Go back to that lesson and try again on Saturday.

A phenom is not a kid who never misses. A phenom is a kid who comes back.

Hunter

## L7 · day 50

- **Subject:** When your child stalls
- **Preview:** What Whole Brain parents do in the dip.
- **Link, in the P.S.:** Open Stage 1 ({{link_stage1}})

Hi {{first_name}},

At some point, your child will stall.

The same lesson three nights in a row. A boss that won't fall. A flat "I don't want to." Stalls are normal, and they pass. Often it's right after a day when nothing seemed to happen.

Here is what Whole Brain parents do.

- **Keep the time. Shrink the work.** Same slot, shorter drill. The streak is what counts.
- **Go back one lesson.** Redo the last one that felt easy. Then come forward.
- **Say less.** No speech about trying hard. Just "Same as yesterday."
- **Stop on tears.** "We're done for today. Good work. Same time tomorrow."

For some kids, the stall comes with borrowing. The rule never changes. Add the friend on this rod, then borrow one from the left. Say it out loud. Slow is fine.

When I trained, I guessed wrong a lot. No one told me what came next. Your child has you.

Hunter

P.S. An easy old lesson is a good warm-up on a stall day. Stage 1 is always open: {{link_stage1}}
