# Stage 6 · Wizard: multiplication

Seven lessons on times on the soroban. It is times tables plus adding, and your child already adds fast. The one new skill is knowing where each small answer lands.

## Where this stage goes

**The level goal.** Multiply 2- and 3-digit numbers by 1- and 2-digit numbers on the soroban, and 2-digit by 1-digit in the head.

**In plain words.** Multiply on the beads, and two-digit times one-digit in the head. Times tables turn into a tool instead of a chore.

**How long.** 6–8 weeks at fifteen minutes a day, six days a week.

Every times problem breaks into small facts. 34 × 7 is 3 tens times 7 and 4 ones times 7. On the soroban you add each small answer in its place, left to right, and the full answer builds itself on the beads.

Write every small answer as two digits: 3 × 7 is 21, and 2 × 4 is 08. The right-hand digit lands on the rod of its place. The left-hand digit lands one rod to the left. That is the whole rule.

Before any of that, the times tables up to 9 × 9 must be instant. The first lesson checks them. If they are not instant yet, spend a week on that lesson alone. It pays for itself in every lesson after.

### The drills in this stage

- **Times tables.** Twenty facts against the clock, every day until they are instant.
- **Written.** The problem stays on paper and only the answer goes on the beads. 2-digit and 3-digit numbers times 1-digit and 2-digit numbers.
- **Mental.** 2-digit times 1-digit in the head, and five 2-digit lines to keep the adding picture sharp.

### Accuracy before speed

The times tables are the one place where speed is the target from day one: twenty facts in a minute, none wrong. Everywhere else the rule is the same as always. A wrong small answer in the right place is still a wrong answer, so check the landing rods before the speed.

### Where this sits on the abacus ladders

Wizard and Champion are the Construction level in our map to UCMAS. The Japanese soroban exams include times and divide papers, and the 1st kyu exam has sixty of those problems. On the trainer's Times ladder, this stage covers rungs 1, 2, 3, 5 and 7: tables, then 2-digit times 1-digit in the head, then 3-digit times 1-digit, then 2-digit times 2-digit.

> ✎ Times tables five minutes a day for two weeks before the first multiply lesson pays off.

## Lesson 6.1 · Times tables: the spell book

*Lesson 43 of 76. Stage 6, lesson 1 of 7. About 10 minutes. You need a pencil and a stopwatch.*

**By the end, your child can** say any times-table fact up to 9 × 9 without counting, twenty in a minute.

### The move

Twenty facts, under a minute, none wrong. This is the spell book every later lesson reads from.

If a fact is slow, it goes on a card. Five minutes a day on the cards, for two weeks, is the usual cost. Most children have a few stubborn ones.

Keep this lesson in the Friday quest until it passes three days in a row.

**Worked example.** Four facts to say out loud, fast.

7 × 8 = 56. 6 × 9 = 54. 8 × 4 = 32. 7 × 6 = 42.

> ✎ Put the slow facts on cards and keep the cards by the breakfast table.

## Lesson 6.2 · 2-digit × 1-digit

*Lesson 44 of 76. Stage 6, lesson 2 of 7. About 15 minutes. You need your soroban, with the problem on paper and only the answer on the beads.*

**By the end, your child can** times a 2-digit number by a 1-digit number on the soroban, left digit first.

### The move

Start with the left digit. Its small answer lands one rod to the left of its place, and the next small answer lands one rod to the right of that.

Keep the problem on paper and only the answer on the beads. This is how experts do it, and it is how times in the head works later.

Say each small answer as two digits. That one habit stops most landing mistakes.

**Worked example.** 34 × 7, left digit first.

- 3 × 7 = 21. It lands on the hundreds and tens rods. Beads: [beads: 210].
- 4 × 7 = 28. It lands on the tens and ones rods. Beads: [beads: 238].
- Read it: **238**.

> ✎ Ask "which rods?" before each small answer goes on, and let your child point.

### Drill

Keep the problem on paper and only the answer on the beads. Left digit first. Write the answer. **Pass mark:** 9 of 10 right. Not yet? Same page tomorrow.

## Lesson 6.3 · 3-digit × 1-digit

*Lesson 45 of 76. Stage 6, lesson 3 of 7. About 15 minutes. You need your soroban, with the problem on paper and only the answer on the beads.*

**By the end, your child can** times a 3-digit number by a 1-digit number on the soroban.

### The move

Same rule, one more digit. Each digit of the top number makes one small answer, and each one lands one rod further right than the last.

Read the answer only at the end. Halfway through, the beads show a number that means nothing yet.

**Worked example.** 386 × 7, one small answer at a time.

- 3 × 7 = 21. It lands on the thousands and hundreds rods. Beads: [beads: 2100].
- 8 × 7 = 56. It lands on the hundreds and tens rods. Beads: [beads: 2660].
- 6 × 7 = 42. It lands on the tens and ones rods. Beads: [beads: 2702].
- Read it: **2,702**.

> ✎ If the answer is off by a lot, check the first landing, because one rod out moves everything.

## Lesson 6.4 · 2-digit × 2-digit

*Lesson 46 of 76. Stage 6, lesson 4 of 7. About 20 minutes. You need your soroban, with the problem on paper and only the answer on the beads.*

**By the end, your child can** times a 2-digit number by a 2-digit number on the soroban.

### The move

Four small answers now. Take the left digit of the top number through both digits of the bottom number. Then the right digit through both.

Each step moves one rod to the right. The pattern is the whole lesson: left digit, both partners, then the next digit, both partners.

Expect this page to be slow at first. That is fine. Four small answers in the right places is a lot to track.

**Worked example.** 34 × 27, four small answers.

- 3 × 2 = 06. It lands on the thousands and hundreds rods. Beads: [beads: 600].
- 3 × 7 = 21. It lands on the hundreds and tens rods. Beads: [beads: 810].
- 4 × 2 = 08. It lands on the hundreds and tens rods. Beads: [beads: 890].
- 4 × 7 = 28. It lands on the tens and ones rods. Beads: [beads: 918].
- Read it: **918**.

> ✎ Let your child say the pattern out loud as they go, because the pattern is what carries over to the head.

### Drill

Keep the problem on paper and only the answer on the beads. Left digit first. Write the answer. **Pass mark:** 8 of 10 right. Not yet? Same page tomorrow.

## Lesson 6.5 · Mental: 2-digit × 1-digit

*Lesson 47 of 76. Stage 6, lesson 5 of 7. About 12 minutes. You need no soroban. A pencil for the answers only.*

**By the end, your child can** times a 2-digit number by a 1-digit number in the head.

### The move

Now the same on the mental beads. 47 × 6: 4 × 6 = 24 lands on the hundreds and tens. Picture 240. Then 7 × 6 = 42 lands on the tens and ones. Add it on the picture. Say 282.

Keep the answer on the beads, not in words. If your child hears themselves saying "two hundred and forty" in their head, that is words. Go back to the picture.

**Worked example.** 47 × 6 on the mental beads.

- 4 × 6 = 24. It lands on the hundreds and tens rods. Beads: [beads: 240].
- 7 × 6 = 42. It lands on the tens and ones rods. Beads: [beads: 282].
- Read it: **282**.

> ✎ This is past what most adults can do without paper, and it is fine to tell your child so.

### Drill

No soroban. Work each small product on the mental beads, left to right. Write only the answer. **Pass mark:** 8 of 10 right. Not yet? Same page tomorrow.

## Lesson 6.6 · 3-digit × 2-digit

*Lesson 48 of 76. Stage 6, lesson 6 of 7. About 20 minutes. You need your soroban, with the problem on paper and only the answer on the beads.*

**By the end, your child can** times a 3-digit number by a 2-digit number on the soroban.

### The move

Six small answers. Take the 3, then the 8, then the 6, each through the 4 and then the 7. Keep a steady rhythm and the answer appears.

This is the longest problem in the course so far. Break the page into two sittings if it helps. The pass mark is the same either way.

- 6 × 7 = 42. It lands on the tens and ones rods. Beads: [beads: 18142].
