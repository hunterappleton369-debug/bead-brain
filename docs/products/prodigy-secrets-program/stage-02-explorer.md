# Stage 2 · Explorer: small friends

Eight lessons on the first real trick: what to do when a rod has no free bead. Small friends make 5, and they turn a stuck rod into a quick trade.

## Where this stage goes

**The level goal.** Use all eight small-friend (5) formulas without thinking, in 2-digit problems.

**In plain words.** Use the "make 5" trick to add and subtract two-digit numbers on the beads. First real technique. The hands start to move without thinking.

**How long.** 3–4 weeks at fifteen minutes a day, six days a week.

A rod has only four bottom beads. So what happens at 4 + 1, when there is no fifth bead to push? You trade. Bring the top bead down for 5, and give back what you did not need. That trade is a small friend.

Two numbers that make 5 are small friends: 1 and 4, 2 and 3. There are eight small-friend moves, four to add and four to take away. This stage teaches them one pair at a time, then mixes them into 2-digit sums, then adds a clock.

It ends with the first sums done in the head, on one rod, with free beads only.

### The drills in this stage

- **One rod.** A start number and one digit. Your child decides: free beads, or friend? Three in every ten are free, on purpose.
- **Written.** Columns of four or five 2-digit numbers on the soroban, left to right.
- **Timed.** The speed round. Same columns, with the time written down each day.
- **Dictation.** You read a column out loud and your child works it on the beads with nothing written.
- **Mental.** Three small numbers in the head, free beads only.

### Accuracy before speed

Every one-rod drill passes at 9 out of 10. The speed round is the first page with a clock, and even there the rule holds: beat the time only while 8 of 10 stay right. If the score drops, slow down. The clock waits.

### Where this sits on the abacus ladders

Explorer is still the Foundation level in UCMAS terms, the part of the course where the formulas are learned one family at a time. It sits before 10th kyu on the Japanese soroban ladder. On the Bead Score, the mental lesson at the end is the first rungs of the ladder: small numbers in the head, no crossing ten.

### The 8 moves of this stage

In this book these moves are orange. Adds first, then take-aways.

| Move | On the beads | Say it |
|---|---|---|
| Plus 1 | +1 = +5 −4 | add 5, take away 4 |
| Plus 2 | +2 = +5 −3 | add 5, take away 3 |
| Plus 3 | +3 = +5 −2 | add 5, take away 2 |
| Plus 4 | +4 = +5 −1 | add 5, take away 1 |
| Minus 1 | −1 = −5 +4 | take away 5, add 4 |
| Minus 2 | −2 = −5 +3 | take away 5, add 3 |
| Minus 3 | −3 = −5 +2 | take away 5, add 2 |
| Minus 4 | −4 = −5 +1 | take away 5, add 1 |

> ✎ When they hesitate, ask "free beads, or friend?" and wait. Do not touch the beads.

## Lesson 2.1 · +1 and +4: friends 1 and 4

*Lesson 9 of 76. Stage 2, lesson 1 of 8. About 12 minutes. You need your soroban and a grown-up beside you.*

**By the end, your child can** add 1 to a rod showing 4, and add 4 when fewer than four beads are free, using the friend move.

### The move

Set 4 on a rod. Now add 1. All four bottom beads are already up. There is no free bead.

So trade. Bring the top bead down. That is plus 5. But you only wanted plus 1, so push four bottom beads away. Plus 5, take away 4. That is the first small friend.

Plus 4 works the same way when there are not four free beads. Bring the top bead down, take one bead away.

The drill mixes in free-bead moves on purpose. Your child has to decide every time. That decision is the skill.

**Worked example.** Start at 3. Add 4, then 1.

- Start with [beads: 3].
- **+ 4.** Ones rod, plus 4: add 5, take away 1. Now [beads: 7].
- **+ 1.** Ones rod, plus 1: the beads are free. Now [beads: 8].
- Read it: **8**.

> ✎ When your child stalls, ask "free beads, or friend?" and then wait longer than feels right.

### Drill

Set the first number on one rod. Then add the second. Free beads, or friend? Write what the rod shows. **Pass mark:** 9 of 10 right. Not yet? Same page tomorrow.

## Lesson 2.2 · +2 and +3: friends 2 and 3

*Lesson 10 of 76. Stage 2, lesson 2 of 8. About 12 minutes. You need your soroban and a grown-up beside you.*

**By the end, your child can** use the friend moves for plus 2 and plus 3.

### The move

Same trade, new pair. Plus 2 when fewer than two beads are free: top bead down, take three away. Plus 3 when fewer than three are free: top bead down, take two away.

2 and 3 are friends because they make 5. That is all your child needs to remember. If you know the friend, you know the move.

Say it out loud each time: "plus 3, add 5, take away 2." The words fade on their own once the hand knows the way.

## Lesson 2.3 · Small-friend adds in 2-digit numbers

*Lesson 11 of 76. Stage 2, lesson 3 of 8. About 15 minutes. You need your soroban and a grown-up beside you.*

**By the end, your child can** add columns of 2-digit numbers that need small friends on either rod.

### The move

Now the friends turn up inside real sums. Each rod gets the same question, tens first: free beads, or friend?

Nothing new happens on any rod. The only new thing is that the trade can come on the tens rod, the ones rod, or both, in the same line.

Use the real soroban. Say each move as you go. Write only the final total.

## Lesson 2.4 · −1 and −4: taking away with friends

*Lesson 12 of 76. Stage 2, lesson 4 of 8. About 12 minutes. You need your soroban and a grown-up beside you.*

**By the end, your child can** take away 1 from a rod showing 5, and take away 4 when four bottom beads are not there.

### The move

Set 5. Now take away 1. The only bead on the beam is the top bead. There is no bottom bead to remove.

Trade the other way. Push the top bead up. That is minus 5. You only wanted minus 1, so give back 4: push four bottom beads up. Minus 5, add 4.

Minus 4 is the same with a different friend. Top bead up, one bead back.

**Worked example.** Start at 5. Take away 1, then 4.

- **− 1.** Ones rod, minus 1: take away 5, add 4. Now [beads: 4].
- **− 4.** Ones rod, minus 4: the beads are there. Now [beads: 0].

## Lesson 2.6 · All eight friends, mixed

*Lesson 14 of 76. Stage 2, lesson 6 of 8. About 15 minutes. You need your soroban and a grown-up beside you.*

**By the end, your child can** work 4-line columns of 2-digit numbers that mix plus and minus, using all eight small friends.

### The move

All eight friends in one drill. Plus and minus mixed, two digits, four lines.

One rule is still missing: what to do when a rod goes past 9 or below 0. That is Stage 3. None of these sums need it, so if a rod looks like it will overflow, check the move again.

This is the page to slow down on. Fast and wrong teaches the wrong move. Slow and right teaches the right one.

## Lesson 2.7 · Speed round

*Lesson 15 of 76. Stage 2, lesson 7 of 8. About 10 minutes. You need your soroban and a grown-up beside you.*

**By the end, your child can** work ten 5-line columns with small friends inside the time, with 8 or more right.

### The move

Accuracy first, then speed. This is the first drill with a clock. Ten columns, five lines each.

Time the whole sheet. Write the time at the top. Do the same sheet tomorrow and try to beat it, but only while 8 or more stay right. A faster time with 7 right does not count.

Speed comes from the hand, not from rushing. Fingers stay close to the beads. Eyes read the next line while the hand finishes the last.

> ✎ Write each day's time on the fridge, because children love watching a number fall.

## Lesson 2.8 · Mental: three small numbers

*Lesson 16 of 76. Stage 2, lesson 8 of 8. About 8 minutes. You need no soroban. A pencil for the answers only.*

**By the end, your child can** add three small numbers in the head on a pictured rod, free beads only.

### The move

Put the soroban away. Picture one empty rod. Add the numbers on the beads in your head.

These sums use free beads only, so there is nothing tricky. The only job is to keep the picture.

If the picture fades, tap the table with thumb and index finger as if the beads were there. Moving the fingers keeps the picture alive. Studies of mental abacus users show the same thing: stop the hands and the sums slow down.

**Worked example.** Picture an empty rod. Add 3, then 5, then 1.

- Start with [beads: 0].
- **+ 3.** Ones rod, plus 3: the beads are free. Now [beads: 3].
- **+ 5.** Ones rod, plus 5: the beads are free. Now [beads: 8].
- **+ 1.** Ones rod, plus 1: the beads are free. Now [beads: 9].
- Read it: **9**.

> ✎ Put the soroban out of sight, and say only "yes" or "try again" at the end.

### Drill

No soroban. Read the column, move the beads in your head, write only the answer. **Pass mark:** 9 of 10 right. Not yet? Same page tomorrow.

## What the trainer adds to this stage

Small friends are where the trainer earns its keep. Each move has a short animation: the top bead comes down, the friend beads go up, in the order the hand should do them. Watching it twice saves a lot of explaining.

The drills also grade as your child goes and offer a Hint button that shows the move. On paper, you are the Hint button, and the one hint is a question: "free beads, or friend?"

The trainer also keeps your child's best result on each drill, so a new best shows up on the screen as soon as it happens.
