# Stage 7 · Champion: division

Seven lessons on dividing, on the beads and in the head, plus the first 3-digit head sums and 2-digit flash. After this stage your child has all four operations.

## Where this stage goes

**The level goal.** Divide by 1- and 2-digit numbers on the soroban, divide by 1-digit numbers in the head, and start 3-digit mental sums.

**In plain words.** Divide on the beads and in the head. Three-digit sums in the head begin. All four operations. Most adults cannot do this.

**How long.** 6–8 weeks at fifteen minutes a day, six days a week.

Dividing is times run backwards. Guess a digit. Times it. Take it away. Repeat. Everything it needs is already in your child's hands.

Take 952 ÷ 7. How many 7s fit in 9? One. Take away 7 hundreds. Two hundreds are left, and with the tens that makes 25 tens. How many 7s in 25? Three. Take away 21 tens. Four tens are left, and with the ones that makes 42. Six 7s. The answer is 136.

On the soroban, set the number to divide on the right and take away as you go. If a guess is too big, it will not come off the beads. Drop the guess by one and try again. That is normal, not a mistake.

### The drills in this stage

- **Written.** Dividing on the soroban: 2-digit, then 3-digit, then 4-digit numbers, by 1-digit and 2-digit numbers, with remainders.
- **Mental.** Dividing by a 1-digit number in the head, and three 3-digit numbers added in the head.
- **Flash.** Eight 2-digit numbers at 0.7 seconds.

### Accuracy before speed

Dividing is slow at first, and it should be. Every guess that is too big costs a few seconds, and that is how the guessing gets good. Hold the pass marks and leave the clock off the dividing pages. The flash page is the one place to push the beat, and only after 8 of 10 at a slower one.

### Where this sits on the abacus ladders

Champion closes the Construction level in our map to UCMAS. Three 3-digit numbers in the head is Bead Score rung 10. Eight 2-digit numbers flashed at 0.7 seconds is rung 13, around 3rd kyu on the Japanese flash anzan ladder. On the Times ladder, the dividing lessons cover rungs 4, 6 and 8.

> ✎ Division is slow at first. Accuracy, then speed.

## Lesson 7.1 · Divide 2-digit by 1-digit

*Lesson 50 of 76. Stage 7, lesson 1 of 7. About 15 minutes. You need your soroban, with the problem on paper and only the answer on the beads.*

**By the end, your child can** divide a 2-digit number by a 1-digit number on the soroban.

### The move

84 ÷ 7. How many 7s in 8? One, with 1 left over. Bring the 4 down beside it: 14. How many 7s in 14? Two. The answer is 12.

On the beads, set 84. Take away 7 tens. Take away 14. The beads read zero, and the answer is the digits you guessed.

**Worked example.** 84 ÷ 7 on the beads.

- Set 84 on the beads: [beads: 84].
- 7 into 8: 1. Take away 70. Beads: [beads: 14]. Answer so far: 1.
- 7 into 14: 2. Take away 14. Beads: [beads: 0]. Answer so far: 12.
- Read it: **12**.

> ✎ Praise a good guess even when it has to be changed, because guessing well is the skill.

### Drill

Set the number to divide on the right of the soroban. Guess, multiply, take away. Write the answer. **Pass mark:** 9 of 10 right. Not yet? Same page tomorrow.

## Lesson 7.2 · Divide 3-digit by 1-digit

*Lesson 51 of 76. Stage 7, lesson 2 of 7. About 15 minutes. You need your soroban, with the problem on paper and only the answer on the beads.*

**By the end, your child can** divide a 3-digit number by a 1-digit number on the soroban.

### The move

Same steps, one more digit. Guess, times, take away. Then the next digit.

Sometimes the first digit is too small. 426 ÷ 6: 6 does not go into 4. So start with 42. 42 ÷ 6 = 7. Then 6 ÷ 6 = 1. The answer is 71, a 2-digit answer from a 3-digit number.

**Worked example.** 952 ÷ 7 on the beads.

- Set 952 on the beads: [beads: 952].
- 7 into 9: 1. Take away 700. Beads: [beads: 252]. Answer so far: 1.
- 7 into 25: 3. Take away 210. Beads: [beads: 42]. Answer so far: 13.
- 7 into 42: 6. Take away 42. Beads: [beads: 0]. Answer so far: 136.
- Read it: **136**.

> ✎ Ask "how many fit?" and let your child guess out loud, even when the guess is wrong.

## Lesson 7.3 · Remainders

*Lesson 52 of 76. Stage 7, lesson 3 of 7. About 12 minutes. You need your soroban, with the problem on paper and only the answer on the beads.*

**By the end, your child can** divide and say what is left over.

### The move

When nothing more fits, what is left on the beads is the remainder.

Write it after the answer with an r: 135 r 5 means 135, with 5 left over.

A remainder is always smaller than the number you divide by. If it is not, one more fits.

**Worked example.** 950 ÷ 7 on the beads.

- Set 950 on the beads: [beads: 950].
- 7 into 9: 1. Take away 700. Beads: [beads: 250]. Answer so far: 1.
- 7 into 25: 3. Take away 210. Beads: [beads: 40]. Answer so far: 13.
- 7 into 40: 5. Take away 35. Beads: [beads: 5]. Answer so far: 135.
- Read it: **135**, and 5 left on the beads is the remainder.

> ✎ If a remainder is bigger than the divider, just ask "does one more fit?"

### Drill

Set the number to divide on the right of the soroban. Guess, multiply, take away. Write the answer and the remainder. **Pass mark:** 9 of 10 right. Not yet? Same page tomorrow.

## Lesson 7.4 · Divide by 2-digit numbers

*Lesson 53 of 76. Stage 7, lesson 4 of 7. About 20 minutes. You need your soroban, with the problem on paper and only the answer on the beads.*

**By the end, your child can** divide a 4-digit number by a 2-digit number on the soroban.

### The move

Guess with the first digits. For 2,752 ÷ 32, think 27 ÷ 3. That is about 8, so try 8. 32 × 8 = 256. Take 256 off the front of 2,752. 19 is left, and with the last digit that makes 192. 32 × 6 = 192. The answer is 86.

The first guess is often one too big. If it will not come off the beads, drop it by one. This happens to everyone, every day.

**Worked example.** 2,752 ÷ 32 on the beads.

- Set 2,752 on the beads: [beads: 2752].
- 32 does not go into 2. Use the next digit too.
- 32 does not go into 27. Use the next digit too.
- 32 into 275: 8. Take away 2,560. Beads: [beads: 192]. Answer so far: 8.
- 32 into 192: 6. Take away 192. Beads: [beads: 0]. Answer so far: 86.
- Read it: **86**.

> ✎ Say "drop it by one" in a bored voice, because a guess that is too big is part of the method.

## Lesson 7.5 · Mental: divide by 1-digit

**By the end, your child can** divide a 2-digit or 3-digit number by a 1-digit number in the head.

### The move

Guess, times, take away, on the picture. 91 ÷ 7: 7 goes into 9 once, with 2 left. That makes 21. 21 ÷ 7 = 3. Say 13.

Hold the number to divide as beads, and watch them come off as each guess lands.

## Lesson 7.6 · Mental: three 3-digit numbers

*Lesson 55 of 76. Stage 7, lesson 6 of 7. About 10 minutes. You need no soroban. A pencil for the answers only.*

**By the end, your child can** add three 3-digit numbers in the head.

### The move

Three rods in the picture now. Research on mental abacus users puts the limit of the picture at about three or four rods. This is near the edge, and it will feel like it.

Three lines of three digits, adding only. Slow is fine. Correct is the target.

**Worked example.** Picture 386. Add 457, then 229.

- Start with [beads: 386].
- **+ 457.** Hundreds rod, plus 4: add 5, take away 1. Tens rod, plus 5: take away 5, carry 1 to the left. Hundreds rod, the carry (plus 1): the beads are free. Ones rod, plus 7: take away 5, add 2, carry 1. Tens rod, the carry (plus 1): the beads are free. Now [beads: 843].
- **+ 229.** Hundreds rod, plus 2: take away 8, carry 1 to the left. Thousands rod, the carry (plus 1): the beads are free. Tens rod, plus 2: add 5, take away 3. Ones rod, plus 9: take away 1, carry 1 to the left. Tens rod, the carry (plus 1): the beads are free. Now [beads: 1072].
- Read it: **1,072**.

> ✎ Try one yourself before your child does it, and then tell them how far you got.

### Drill

## Lesson 7.7 · Flash: 2-digit numbers

*Lesson 56 of 76. Stage 7, lesson 7 of 7. About 8 minutes. You need a grown-up to show or read the numbers. No soroban, no paper.*

**By the end, your child can** add eight 2-digit numbers flashed at 0.7 seconds each.

### The move

Two-digit flash. Eight numbers at 0.7 seconds.

The numbers go by faster than they can be said. That is the point. Words are too slow. The picture is not.

Each number lands on two rods at once. Thirty-four is a 3 on the tens rod and a 4 on the ones, taken as one shape.

**Worked example.** Eight 2-digit numbers.

- Number 1: **34**. Mental beads: [beads: 34].
- Number 2: **28**. Mental beads: [beads: 62].
- Number 3: **17**. Mental beads: [beads: 79].
- Number 4: **45**. Mental beads: [beads: 124].
- Number 5: **12**. Mental beads: [beads: 136].
- Number 6: **63**. Mental beads: [beads: 199].
- Number 7: **50**. Mental beads: [beads: 249].
- Number 8: **21**. Mental beads: [beads: 270].
- Say **270**.

## What the trainer adds to this stage

The trainer shows each dividing step on the beads: the guess, the take-away, what is left. It also runs the 0.7-second flash rounds at an exact beat, which is faster than a parent can flip cards for long.

The paper pages hold the same problems. For the flash page, read at your fastest clear pace and let the trainer handle the true speed.
